Before a Technician Visits: Essential Pre-Service Checklist

Maintenance Updated: October 2026 • 5 min read

Practical reading for planning repairs, discussing maintenance and asking better service questions before your technician arrives.

How Proper Preparation Saves Time and Prevents Unexpected Costs

When a pipe begins leaking or water pressure suddenly drops, taking ten minutes to observe and record the symptoms can dramatically improve your service experience. A well-prepared homeowner empowers the technician to arrive with the exact fittings and diagnostic tools required.

Key Takeaway

Do not attempt temporary fixes using chemical adhesives or aggressive tightening before the technician inspects the assembly. Document the symptoms with photos or short videos to share upon arrival.

1. Record the Exact Symptoms and Timeline

Note down precisely when the problem was first noticed and under what circumstances it manifests. Is water dripping constantly or only when a nearby appliance (such as a washing machine or shower) runs? Does the pipe make a hammering or banging sound when taps are quickly closed?

✓ Identify Affected Fixtures

Check if adjacent taps or showers on the same floor experience similar pressure losses or drainage delays.

✓ Note Water Clarity & Odour

Observe if the water is discoloured, cloudy, or accompanied by an earthy or metallic smell.

✓ Check Surrounding Moisture

Examine baseboards, plaster walls, and cabinetry for peeling paint, bubbling, or cool damp patches.

✓ Locate Your Isolation Valve

Know where the nearest shutoff stopcock is located so water can be safely isolated if leakage accelerates.

✓ Monitor Meter Movement

Verify whether your water meter dial moves while all household taps are closed to confirm active hidden slab leaks.

✓ Prepare Access Clearance

Clear out storage items, mats, and baskets under vanity cabinets so the technician can position diagnostic tools immediately.

2. Clear the Work Area for Safe Access

Technicians require adequate physical clearance to maneuver pipe cutters, wrenches, pressure gauges, and protective mats. If the issue is under a kitchen sink or bathroom vanity, clear out cleaning bottles, storage baskets, and electronics beforehand. This prevents accidental chemical spills and protects your household belongings.

3. Precision Diagnostic Tools and Preparation

Clean pipe work requires specialized ratcheting shears and deburring cones that deliver smooth, perpendicular cuts without crimping pipe walls. This ensures mechanical compression couplings and push-fit connections seal cleanly without hidden internal stress.

4. Know What Questions to Ask During the Visit

A reputable plumbing professional will always explain their findings before starting physical repair work. When your technician completes their diagnostic check, make sure to ask:

  • What is the root cause of the failure: material age, excessive hydraulic pressure, or mineral buildup?
  • Is a localized repair sufficient, or does this indicate wider pipework fatigue in adjacent joints?
  • What grade of replacement pipe and fittings are being installed (e.g. CPVC, PVC, or GI)?
  • Will you perform a pressure test to certify the seal before packing away your tools?
